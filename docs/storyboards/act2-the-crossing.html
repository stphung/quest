Quest · Act II · Design Storyboard

The Crossing

Zone 50 falls. A signal answers from the roots of Yggdrasil. You burn everything you built to launch a vessel into the void — and ferry a dying world home.

The Loom trembles. Something distant answers.

Dark-shipped · ACT2_ENABLED = false · fully built, invisible by default

00 The shape of it

A second game hiding behind one boolean

Act 1 is 50 zones of combat, prestige, and endgame layers. Act 2 is a different game entirely — a slow, authored, wall-clock voyage that replaces the whole screen. It ships in every build, complete and tested, yet no player has ever seen it.

The kill-switch vessel::ACT2_ENABLED is false in every committed build; QUEST_ACT2=1 reveals it for preview. One detail is deliberately not gated: defeating the Zone 50 boss still records the signal in your save even while Act 2 sleeps — so the day the switch flips, every already-qualified hero lights up at once, no replay required.

01 Sub-project 1 · The launch gate

Everything you have, spent in a single breath

The crossing is not unlocked — it is paid for. Four gates must all be true, and then one irreversible burn sends you off.

All-or-nothing. perform_launch() subtracts the full cost in one action and sets sail. If any gate is unmet it refuses entirely — there is no partial spend, no down payment. Between discovery and launch, an atmospheric whisper surfaces on the ticker every ~60 seconds of play, rotating through the signals from beyond the branches.

02 The voyage clock

Time you cannot rush — only shorten by building

The crossing runs in real wall-clock time. A sea-day passes in about nine real hours; the maiden voyage's ~37 sea-days is roughly two real weeks.

The engine's load-bearing property is offline equivalence: tick() advances lazily in whole game-minutes, so resolving after a week away produces state bitwise-identical to having watched it live. The clock never changes — GAME_MINUTES_PER_REAL_MINUTE = 2.64 is fixed forever. Crossings get faster only because you buy Drive levels. The ramp is earned, never compressed.

2.64 game-min / real-min ~9h real per sea-day ~14 days maiden crossing lazy tick · resolves offline exactly

03 Sub-project 2 · The route

A spine-and-diamond chart in four chapters

38 authored waypoints strung on a directed graph: branches split at junctions and rejoin within the same chapter, each chapter ending at a single gateway. The Tree is the graph's only sink.

I

The Shallows

From The Last Harbor out past the Lightship Vigil, the Drowned Choir, the Kelp Meadows — the world you can still almost see. Ends at The Shallows Gate.

II

The Drift Roads

The Wandering Fair, the Mirrorcalm, Saint Elm's Rest, the Beacon Graveyard — currents with names, where crews are found and letters still reach you.

III

The Starless Deep

Past the Last Lantern the mail stops coming. The Going-Dark: the first arrival here is the night no letter arrives.

IV

The Roots of Light

Root-rivers glow beneath the hull; the water tastes of orchards. A field of blooms closed like fists — until they aren't. Ends at The Tree.

38 waypoints 45 roads 7 junctions 8 rumors every run: ≥5 soul candidates, ≥2 shipyards, ≥2 rest stops

The affordability invariant: at every junction the cheapest road out never costs more than 25 provisions — proven in tests — so running your hold dry always means drifting in place for 36 hours, never getting stranded.

04 The one dial

Trim: how you choose to carry the loss

A single posture dial composes with wind and crew into leg-time and provisions burn. It's a mood as much as a mechanic.

Run fastest

Every leg arrives sooner — and the hold empties faster to do it. Hungry sailing.

Cruise default

The balanced posture. What she sails at when you aren't deciding otherwise.

Quiet thrifty

Slower and leaner — and the only trim that hears silence-banks, buying a rumor from each.

Mourn slowest

The thriftiest of all. After a full day at sea in Mourn, hope rises — grief made into wind.

05 Two gauges

Provisions burn. Hope is the wind.

Provisions 100 / 100

The hold. Burns while traveling — composed from trim × the tender's station × the night's weather. Empty mid-road means you drift, not sink.

Hope 7 · bright

A small number with a name, never a percentage (0–10, launches at 7). Its one mechanical effect is speed — hope is literally the wind.

When hope goes ashen it enters the Long Silence: arcs pause, every leg crawls at the worst rate, and only a rest stop can relight it. You can be worn down into the Silence — you can never buy your way in.

06 Sub-project 3 · The crew

Eight souls compete for seven seats

Three launch with you; five are found along the roads. Each carries an affinity that strengthens one station, a voice for junction counsel, and a three-beat arc that pays out on a rest-day timer.

Stations — Helm shortens legs, Tender saves rations, Watch turns the nights. You can only post seven.
Farewell — put someone ashore to free a seat, remembered, at a cost of 1 hope.
The covenant — a soul can only be lost in an authored scene. No tick, no roll, no dice ever takes one from you. Loss is never random.

07 Weather & nights

The void's moods, computed from a single seed

Weather (Current, Silence-Bank, Squall) is a pure function of (voyage_seed, hour). Nights are typed per day from (voyage_seed, day) — Quiet, Cold, Hungry, Singing, Strange.

Because nothing is stored, offline resolution and live play always agree to the letter. Who stands the watch decides how each night prices out — the Watch station reading directly into the outcome matrix.

weather: pure(seed, hour) nights: pure(seed, day) no stored state · always reproducible

09 Letters from home & the going-dark

The world you left, writing back

Twelve sequenced letters arrive at ports through Chapters I and II — never on a timer, always delivered when you make landfall. They are the emotional throughline: the harbor, the guild, the fisher-fleets, the Loom-tenders, the children.

— the children of the harbor
We drew your ship. Aldi made the sails too big and will not fix it, but so is a whale and nobody is scared of THOSE anymore.
— the Loom-tenders
The Loom gave us one more spool and went quiet. Not cold — quiet. The weave says you are nearly there. Trust the weave. We always did.
— everyone · the Last Letter, handed over at the Threshold
This one is signed by all of us — warden and guild, fleets and children. You have carried us this far.
No letters. There will be no more letters.

Past the Last Lantern, the first arrival is the night the mail does not come. From there you cross in silence.

10 The finale

“The crossing is over. The arrival has just begun.”

The Tree — after the drowned parishes, the fair, the starless deep. Lines are thrown. There are hands to catch them; there is a bell. Reaching the Tree fires take_finale_playback() once and sets vessel_arrived — the exact hook a future Act 3 would key off, the way Act 2 keys off launch.

11 Sub-project 5 · The colony & the ferry loop

The crossing is the run. The Colony is the campaign.

Arrival isn't the end — it's the first delivery. The maiden voyage is played by hand; every crossing after is a hands-off ferry run, carrying souls from a dying world of 100,000 to the Tree, one load at a time.

100k
souls in the dying world's pool
~19
crossings across the era
~3 mo
real-time, skilled play
1.1%
of the waiting world lost per crossing — the dark's toll

Each landfall pays Salvage, spent on two yards — and choosing between them is the whole loop:

Drive

Every level multiplies future sail-time by 0.70, compounding toward 20× top speed. Faster turnarounds — but the dark bites once per crossing.

Shipwright

Every level grows the hold ×1.36. Bigger loads mean fewer crossings mean fewer tolls — more of the world carried home.

Speed versus salvation — and the margin is meant to be wide:

Reckless — Drive only

~54% saved

~85 near-empty crossings; the world bleeds to the dark while you sprint.

Skilled — hold first

~87% saved

Lean into the hold, just enough Drive to keep her quick. Most of the world comes home.

Skill is rewarded, not marginal — a 33-point swing on the only number that ever rises. The ramp is a buildup then a fast-fun stretch: a two-week maiden voyage, then ~14 → ~3 real-day turnarounds while the loads climb into the thousands.
